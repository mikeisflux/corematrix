# ForeverComicCon: what I would build next

Written as if this were my show and my business. The first block is already
in the hall; the rest is ordered by how much it would move the numbers that
matter (people staying, people coming back, booths sold) per week of work.

## In the hall now

- **People to people.** Click anyone with a green triangle to open a private
  chat (only the two of you receive it); the 👥 panel lists everyone on the
  floor with wave / message / walk-over, your chat threads and unread counts.
  Hall-chat and private messages show as speech bubbles over the avatar.
- **Emotes and reactions.** Eight animated emotes (keys 1–8 or the 🙌 panel)
  and eight emoji reactions; everyone nearby sees them.
- **Guests have names.** Visitors can set a display name without an account,
  so chat is not "Visitor" talking to "Visitor".
- **Meet-up links.** "Copy a link to where I'm standing" and "Send where I am"
  in a chat; opening the link walks the other person to that spot.
- **Minimap** (M): the whole hall with you, live people and ConBot; click to
  walk there.
- **First-visit tips** card, different on touch screens.
- **Phones.** Joystick bottom-left (push to the rim to run), one-finger look,
  panels as a bottom sheet, slim top rail, no pinch-zoom fights, desktop
  cards hidden.
- **Theater** with uploads, downsampling, resumable uploads and synced playback.

## Next, in order

1. **Reasons to come back daily.** A daily "floor pass" stamp (coins) for
   walking past three booths you have not seen, a weekly scavenger hunt
   (find the five marked booths, win a badge), and a visible streak on the
   avatar's badge. The economy already has coins and streaks; this gives
   them a reason.
2. **Exhibitor presence.** Let a booth owner "be at the booth": while they
   are standing in it their booth gets a glowing ring and a "owner here, say
   hi" tag, and visitors near the booth see a one-tap "ask the owner" chat.
   This is the thing a real con floor has that a website does not.
3. **Scheduled moments.** A programme board by the arcade: "Trailer premiere
   8 pm", "Artist X signing at booth 1702 at 3 pm". The theater already keeps
   everyone in sync, so a premiere with 40 avatars on the benches is one
   setting away. Push the schedule to the weekly email that already exists.
4. **Voice at the benches.** Opt-in proximity voice (WebRTC, groups of up
   to eight within 25 ft). Text bubbles are good; a chattering crowd around a
   screening is what keeps people for an hour.
5. **Booth analytics people can act on.** The dashboard shows views and
   clicks; add "who walked past vs who stopped vs who clicked", heat by hour,
   and a one-line suggestion ("your banner stops 4% of passers-by; booths
   with a face on the banner average 9%").
6. **Mobile app shell.** A PWA manifest with install prompt, landscape lock
   in walk mode, and haptics on E/emotes gets most of the "app" feel without
   an app store.
7. **Friends and follows.** Follow an exhibitor or a person; a small feed
   ("3 people you follow are on the floor") on the People panel and in the
   email digest.
8. **Photo mode.** Freeze, frame, add the booth name and date, one-tap share
   image. Every share is an ad for the show with a link back to that booth.
9. **Guest → account nudges.** A guest who has chatted, waved or walked for
   ten minutes gets one soft prompt to keep their name, coins and friends.
10. **Moderation basics before the crowd grows.** Mute/report on a person,
    an admin page listing reports with the last 20 messages, word filter on
    chat and private messages. Cheap now, expensive later.

## Smaller polish worth an afternoon each

- Footsteps and a low room tone, muted by default, one toggle in the gear menu.
- Booth "open hours" ribbon from the exhibitor's time zone.
- Avatar idle variety (look at phone, stretch) so crowds read as alive.
- Keyboard shortcut sheet (?) and a controller mapping.
- A "walk to" from search results instead of the camera fly only.
